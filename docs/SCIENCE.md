# Science writeup — Dynamical Spacetime

## Research question

How does a perturbed curved spacetime dynamically evolve, and what physical information about mass, spin, amplitude and wavelength is encoded in the gravitational radiation it produces?

## Motivation

Newtonian simulations answer “where does the object go?” General relativity answers “how does the geometry through which everything moves respond to energy and momentum?” Linearized gravitational perturbations on Schwarzschild have an exact reduction to a 1+1 wave equation (Regge–Wheeler, Zerilli). Black-hole ringdown is a spectrum of quasinormal modes. Binary inspiral is constrained by post-Newtonian energy balance. Those three facts are enough to build a laboratory that is both visually extreme and scientifically honest.

## Hypotheses (testable)

1. Small-amplitude RW/Zerilli evolutions radiate with energy \(\propto \varepsilon^2\) (linear theory).
2. A quadratic source \(\lambda\Psi^2\) produces a measurable excess of energy and extra spectral peaks above a threshold \(\varepsilon_\star\).
3. Increasing remnant spin raises the real QNM frequency and shrinks ISCO and photon-orbit radii, matching Kerr formulae.
4. Unequal mass ratio lowers peak strain and remnant spin relative to \(q=1\) at fixed total mass.
5. Grid doubling yields a positive observed convergence order in radiated-energy proxies.
6. Matched-template inversion recovers total mass more accurately than individual spins (degeneracy).

## What would count as a result

Not “we made a black hole on a screen.” A result is a measured scaling \(E_{\rm rad}(\varepsilon)\), a threshold \(\varepsilon_\star\), a QNM residual vs spin, a mismatch \(\mathcal{M}\) for the inverse problem, and a convergence order \(p\).

## Ethical / competition scope

No human subjects, no vertebrate animals, no hazardous chemicals. Pure computational physics. Team size ≤ 3. Research window ≤ 12 months for ISEF.
